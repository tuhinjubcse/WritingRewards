The old Dodge rattled down the winding cliffside road toward the beach, its worn shocks protesting each pothole. Jane gripped the sun-cracked vinyl seat, glancing over at Mark behind the wheel. His brow was furrowed, focused on navigating the treacherous turns through the coastal fog. The radio played softly, an old jazz tune Jane vaguely recognized filling the silence between them. She felt a pang of longing for the familiar, although over the past month at the residency she had grown accustomed to the isolated quiet of her studio and sparse encounters with others at meals. 

Jane thought back to the first time she had seen Mark, a month ago at the welcome dinner for the artist residency. He had been quietly confident, making wry observations about the eccentric group of creatives thrown together in communal living. His sardonic humor and reticent thoughtfulness had drawn her in immediately. However, her shyness held her back from striking up a conversation with him that night. She chided herself for her hesitance now, aware of the precious little time remaining before their inevitable parting in two days. 

For weeks, they had been cloistered at the artists' residency, tucked away in the woods and working in solitude. The sprawling main house and studios sat on a hundred acres of forest, an escape from the tempo of ordinary life. Meals were the only times they regularly crossed paths. Polite greetings were exchanged, discussions of work and process, but little else. There was an unspoken charge between them, a kind of heat—at least Jane felt it in his lingering glances and the twitch of a smile he allowed himself when they made eye contact. She found excuses to linger in the communal spaces, hoping for a stolen moment or private word with Mark. Their encounters were fleeting but fueled her longing. During one dinner, as candlelight flickered across the table, Mark asked Jane about her own work. She explained her use of dramatic colors and abstractions to convey emotion, while he nodded thoughtfully, dark eyes peering intently at her.
 
Jane felt the residency was ending too quickly. Over the past month, she had started and scrapped three paintings, unable to focus because of her preoccupation with Mark. Her thoughts kept drifting to his dark eyes, his deliberate way of considering a question before answering, the glimpses of humor he allowed to surface when recounting some absurdist tale of life in the city. She felt anchored to him by invisible threads, being reeled in bit by bit with each furtive look across the mess hall. The hushed, intimate dinners they had shared, making eye contact across the worn farm table, left her breathless in a way she hadn't felt for years.

The residency was ending in two days. This would likely be their last excursion together. Jane had suggested heading to the beach to escape the claustrophobia of the communal space, filled with the detritus of temporary inhabitants about to disperse. Mark had agreed with uncharacteristic eagerness that surprised and delighted her. After a month of quick, awkward encounters, his sudden enthusiasm gave her a burst of hope that had been absent before. 

The Dodge emerged from the woods into a sandy clearing. The fog parted to reveal the wide blue ocean stretched endlessly before them, azure waters lapping at ivory sands. Mark pulled the car up to the shore and cut the engine. They set up their towels and umbrella in silence, the anxious energy between them palpable. Jane felt self-conscious in her swimsuit under Mark’s appreciative gaze and busied herself arranging their provisions, fighting the urge to cover up.She walked down to the soothing sound of waves lapping at the shore. The beach stretched before Jane, a pale curve of sand giving way to emerald swells. 

Jane slipped off her sundress, acutely aware of Mark's appreciative gaze on her as she did so, and walked toward the surf in her swimsuit. The chilled water was bracing, raising goosebumps on her bare skin. She dove under the next wave breaking, cool water washing the heat and tension from her body. Surfacing with a gasp, Jane began to find a steady rhythm in the sea. The saltwater cleared her mind, but also heightened her senses, aware of Mark waiting for her on the beach. She drifted between fantasies of what was still possible between them to agonizing over the missed opportunities and their imminent separation. Everything seemed dreamlike and hazy in the fog, as if this moment might dissolve into the ether at any moment.

The hushed, intimate dinners they had shared, making eye contact across the worn farm table, left her breathless in a way she hadn't felt for years. 

When she returned to their encampment, Mark was reading under the umbrella. His shy smile and lingering embrace as he wrapped her in a towel and handed her a thermos of coffee gave her hope that this day might lead to new beginnings for them. They sat together under the banner of shade for hours, gazing out at the sea, conversing more freely than ever before. Jane opened up extensively about her work, her past, her family, and her hopes while Mark listened intently, drawing out more details with his astute questions. She was surprised by their easy rapport, how fully she felt seen and how at ease she felt opening up to Mark. He recounted amusing anecdotes from past relationships, travel misadventures, eccentric neighbors and life in the city. His wry observations and witty stories kept her spellbound throughout the long, golden afternoon. 

As the sun began to set, they realized with disappointment that they would have to head back soon to the residency. Jane suggested a walk along the shore to stretch their legs and make the most of their remaining time together. Hand in hand they meandered down the winding coastline, waves gently lapping at their feet. An easy intimacy developed between them as they wandered together, talking for hours it seemed. The sea and sky were awash in orange and pink, as if the world itself was aflame. 

They returned to the old Dodge as night fell, damp sand clinging to their bare feet. Jane felt both buoyant at the deep connection formed with Mark today and bereft at its impermanence. The drive back to the residency was quiet but comfortable, no words needed. At the main house, they lingered in a tender embrace. Jane tilted her face up to Mark's, finding his lips in a long, slow kiss. They clung to one another, both wanting more yet aware their time was up. With reluctance, Mark bid her goodnight and they parted, unsure of what tomorrow would bring but grateful for the gift of today.
