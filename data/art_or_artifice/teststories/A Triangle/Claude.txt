The old couple shuffled down the sidewalk, hands clasped, bracing each other against the chilly air. There was something mesmerizing about their slow, unsteady gait, the way they leaned into each other with each step. 

I found myself following behind them, captivated for reasons I couldn't quite place. They turned down a tree-lined street and arrived at a little bungalow with yellow trim. The man fished a set of keys out of his coat pocket and opened the front door, its creak announcing their arrival. As they disappeared inside, I lingered under a large oak across the street. 

Night was falling fast and hard; the sky had turned the ominous gray-green of an approaching storm. The wind started howling, shaking the branches of the trees. Something stirred in my memory - hadn't we passed a large armchair discarded on the sidewalk a few blocks back, its floral upholstery charred and crumbling? There had been something disturbingly familiar about that chair. I searched back beyond the faded memories of my current lifetime, straining further, delving into the deepest recesses of my mind - but the meaning of the chair eluded me. I racked my brain for hours under that oak tree hoping to unlock the secrets behind that armchair.

The blanket of night had descended upon the street.All I knew was that I felt drawn to that house and could not leave until the secrets hidden within revealed themselves. A few lights flickered on in the bungalow. Through lace curtains I could make out the old couple moving about leisurely preparing a home cooked meal, sitting down to eat, laughing and talking with ease and familiarity. A fierce fondness for these strangers swelled in my chest, and for the first time in most of my memory I felt a wave of melancholy and homesickness. 
 

The rain began to pelt me as I hid under the tree. Soaked through and through, I pressed my face up against the cold glass of the window, peering into their home. Their conversation was muted through the glass but I could feel the warmth of their bond, the unconditional love and understanding borne of a lifetime together. A lifetime I had once shared with them, before my quick passing. The discarded armchair lingered at the edge of my thoughts, its ruined remains refusing to be forgotten. 

Rain began to fall, first in wayward spatters then in unfettered sheets. Within minutes, water rushed down the street, pooling around my ankles. I should leave, I knew, head home before the flooding worsened. But I remained anchored to the spot, possessed by an irresistible desire to unlock the meaning of that armchair and understand my connection with the old couple. How had I stumbled upon again these people I loved so deeply in life? Had I finally come home in death?

The rain soaked through my clothes as I shivered in the cold, but still I could not turn away. They finished their meal and drew the curtains. Time stretched on as I stood half-concealed under drooping oak branches, kept company only by the steady crash of rain. What was this hold they had over me? Memories welled up and flooded my senses. I remembered vacations by the sea, laughter over family dinners, quiet moments and deep conversations. A lifetime of memories I thought lost forever in the abyss of death came rushing back to me in that moment. 

As the floodwaters rose to my knees, then my waist, understanding began to dawn - hazy and impossible and yet undeniable. The water rose higher and higher, but my feet remained stuck in place, as if held there by an unseen force awaiting redemption. I knew then with utter certainty that my wandering spirit would find peace only when the last of the secrets hidden within that little yellow house were laid bare. 

The woman offered me a sad, knowing smile. I gazed into her eyes, eyes I knew as well as my own, and in them saw reflected the lifetime we had shared - and lost. My heart swelled near to bursting. And then, with their house crumbling into the hungry flood behind them, my parents turned and walked away into the rain. Leaving me once more alone, clinging to memories of a life now forever lost. The waters continued to rise around me but I could not follow. My spirit remained tethered to the earth, my penance unfinished, my rest unearned. The discarded chair lingered at the edge of my thoughts, a ruined reminder of a past forever out of reach. 
I called out to them but the churning waters muffled my cries. And as the flood swallowed me whole, I awoke with a start in my bed, my heart pounding, tears streaming down my face. The dream had felt so real. I knew then that I had been given a chance to make peace with my past and found closure at last. My restless spirit had come home.
